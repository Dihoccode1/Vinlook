## POI-011 — Thảo Cầm Viên Sài Gòn

English name: Saigon Zoo and Botanical Gardens.

### Narration EN

Saigon Zoo and Botanical Gardens brings together two ways of looking at nature: observing animals and discovering plants. Its story began in eighteen sixty-four, when a nursery and animal enclosures were established here. What began as a project in colonial Saigon became part of the city's longer relationship with gardens, scientific study, and public recreation.

The garden's own history describes the work of collecting and growing trees, including plants native to the region and species brought from elsewhere. It also presents the grounds as a place of memory, where older buildings and mature trees connect different generations of city life. As you explore, consider how a tree can be both a living organism and a reminder of time passing.

The institution describes conservation, wildlife rescue, research, and education as parts of its mission. Those responsibilities offer another perspective on a zoo visit. Looking at an animal can become a starting point for questions about its habitat, the pressures it faces, and the care involved in protecting wildlife.

You do not need to identify every plant or animal to appreciate that connection. Choose a detail that catches your attention: the shape of a leaf, the pattern of branches, or the way an animal moves. Let that small observation guide your curiosity. Here, the history of a city garden and the study of living things can be considered together, one careful look at a time.

## POI-012 — Phố đi bộ Nguyễn Huệ

English name: Nguyen Hue Walking Street.

### Narration EN

Nguyen Hue Walking Street offers a view of Ho Chi Minh City through its public spaces. The broad promenade stretches from the City Hall area towards Bach Dang Wharf. Rather than focusing on a single monument, this stop invites you to notice the relationship between the open street and the buildings around it.

The national tourism authority describes a setting framed by high rises, restaurants, shopping arcades, and boutiques. At the northern end stands City Hall. Look at the different outlines of the surrounding buildings and consider how each contributes to the view. A public promenade can make those differences easier to notice, giving you space to pause rather than simply pass through.

The tourism authority also describes Nguyen Hue as a gathering place for residents and visitors, particularly as evening arrives. That social role is part of the street's appeal: people can share an urban space without having the same destination or reason for being there. You might come to look at architecture, meet a friend, or simply watch the city around you.

For a moment, choose one direction and take in the scene as a whole. Then look again for a smaller detail that you missed at first. Think about the contrast between a wide, open foreground and the varied city skyline. Nguyen Hue offers a useful reminder that experiencing a city can begin with paying attention to the spaces between its landmarks.

## POI-013 — Phố Tây Bùi Viện

English name: Bui Vien Walking Street.

### Narration EN

Bui Vien is associated with a lively side of Ho Chi Minh City's visitor experience. The national tourism authority describes it as a meeting place for backpackers and other people seeking food and entertainment. It became an official walking street in twenty seventeen, adding a formal identity to an area already known to travellers.

Food is one way to understand its international character. The tourism authority describes Vietnamese street dishes alongside food from India, Italy, China, and Thailand. Rather than searching for a single taste that represents the entire street, consider how these different offerings sit beside one another. A visitor district can bring together familiar comforts and opportunities to try something new.

The same account describes restaurants, barbecue places, and street performers as parts of the atmosphere, with activity building around dinner time. Your own visit may be different from that description. Treat what you hear and see as a particular moment, rather than expecting every evening to repeat the same scene.

As you take in the street, think about the exchanges that make a place memorable: choosing a meal, asking a question, or sharing a table. The story here is about people and encounters as much as buildings. You can enjoy that perspective without following a fixed checklist. Pause where it is comfortable, notice the mix of languages and food around you, and let the details of your own visit shape your impression of Bui Vien.

## POI-014 — Địa đạo Củ Chi tại Bến Dược

English name: Cu Chi Tunnels at Ben Duoc.

### Narration EN

This stop concerns Ben Duoc, one part of the Cu Chi Tunnels historical site. It is distinct from Ben Dinh, another section of the wider heritage complex. Keeping those names separate helps place this visit within the larger story of Cu Chi.

Vietnam's Department of Cultural Heritage traces the tunnel system to resistance against French colonial rule around nineteen forty-eight. Initially, short passages provided places to conceal documents and shelter people. The network was reinforced and expanded during the Vietnam War. Its history is therefore a history of change over time, rather than a single construction project completed at once.

The heritage account describes connected passages, branching routes, concealed entrances, and ventilation openings. It also records spaces for storing supplies, preparing food, working, and treating the wounded. These details describe the wider historical system; they do not mean that every feature is available to enter at Ben Duoc today.

Consider what such spaces suggest about daily life under wartime conditions. A passage could be a route between places, but shelter, food, air, and medical care were also essential concerns. Thinking about those needs can make the history more human than a list of tunnel dimensions or military statistics.

As you learn about this place, give yourself time to reflect on both resourcefulness and suffering. Look for the distinction between historical evidence, restored features, and explanations offered during your visit. Each can contribute to understanding, while remaining a different kind of encounter with the past.

## POI-015 — Chùa Bà Thiên Hậu tại Chợ Lớn

English name: Thien Hau Temple in Cholon.

### Narration EN

Thien Hau Temple in Cholon introduces a place where religious practice and community heritage meet. This is the temple associated with the Tue Thanh Assembly Hall, not another temple of the same name elsewhere in the city. The city's tourism promotion centre connects it with the Cantonese Chinese community of Cholon.

Thien Hau is revered as a protective sea goddess. The national tourism information centre relates this devotion to the journeys of Chinese migrants who travelled south by sea. That is an account of religious belief and community memory, rather than a claim that a supernatural event has been historically proven.

The building also rewards attention to detail. Tourism sources describe ceramic figures on the roof, decorative reliefs, and ornamental inscriptions. Look for the relationship between the smaller decorations and the larger architectural form. You might first notice an overall silhouette, then discover that it is made up of many individual shapes and scenes.

As you consider these details, remember that a place of worship can hold different meanings for different people. For one person it may be a familiar spiritual home; for another, a first introduction to a cultural tradition. Both perspectives can encourage careful observation and respect.

Let the visit become an opportunity to think about what communities carry with them: beliefs, artistic skills, shared memories, and ways of gathering. In Cholon, those themes offer a thoughtful way to understand the temple beyond its appearance, while leaving space for the personal meaning it holds for worshippers.
